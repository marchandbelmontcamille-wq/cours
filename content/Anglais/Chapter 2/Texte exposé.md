## Partie 1
The film shows violence in many forms: whippings, torture, hangings, rapes, public humiliation, separation of families and deprivation of all freedom. Some slaves, like Patsey, were regularly subjected to extreme violence: she was whipped to death and sexually abused by her master. The slave market scene also shows the forced nudity and brutal tearing of children from their mothers. All this brutality is never justified in the film. The director films with a lot of detail and special effects such as the blood spatters during the whipping of patsey (photo patsey) to denounce the real cruelty of slavery, based on the historical testimony of Solomon Northup, and forcing the viewer to look reality in the face, without watering it down. The violence thus aims to remind us of the inhumanity of the system and to provoke reflection rather than to shock gratuitously.
## Partie 2
In the film, several white characters illustrate the diversity of attitudes toward slavery. William Ford, the first master, shows himself to be humane and respectful toward Solomon, yet he remains, nonetheless, a slave owner. 
In contrast, Edwin Epps represents the extreme cruelty of the system: he is violent, racist, and abuses his power. 
Bass, a Canadian carpenter, openly denounces slavery and helps Solomon regain his freedom. 
Finally, Epps' wife, jealous of Patsey, shows the cold complicity of white women within the system. 
This diversity reflects the complexity of the time: not all whites are the same, some are complicit, others rebel, and still others remain ambiguous. This allows for a nuanced view of slavery and emphasizes the plurality of behaviors.
